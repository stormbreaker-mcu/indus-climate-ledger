## 1. Executive Summary & Objective

Harshit Sharma's "Climate-Resilience Ledger" hypothesis proposes that the Indus script functioned as a bureaucratic scarcity-management technology during the 4.2 ka mega-drought. While epigraphic patterns in the Interactive Corpus of Indus Texts (ICIT) display a consistent 1.4× to 1.8× enrichment of "Jar + Numeral" rationing motifs in Class-A urban centers, this directional trend fails to reach statistical significance due to a severe sampling imbalance: **2,304 Urban inscriptions** vs. only **210 Peripheral/Rural inscriptions**.

Our post-hoc Monte Carlo simulations demonstrate that this test is severely underpowered, carrying an **84.2% chance of a false negative (Type II error)**. To achieve the standard 80% statistical power threshold for the Co-occurrence variant, the peripheral corpus must be expanded to **1,757 inscriptions**—a net addition of **1,547 inscriptions** from rural and intermediate settlements.
